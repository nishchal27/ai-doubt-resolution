Human Digestive System

Chapter: Human Digestive System

The digestive system in humans is a group of organs that work together to break down and absorb food. The alimentary canal (or gastrointestinal tract) is a long tube along which food passes and digestion takes place. The major parts of the alimentary canal are the mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine and large intestine.

Mouth: Food is taken into the mouth where it is chewed by teeth and mixed with saliva. Saliva contains the enzyme amylase which begins the digestion of starch to simpler sugars. The tongue helps in mixing the food and in swallowing.

Teeth: Different types of teeth are present in the mouth — incisors, canines, premolars and molars. They help in cutting, tearing and grinding of food. Teeth are embedded in the jaw and are made of dentine and enamel.

Oesophagus and Stomach

Oesophagus: The oesophagus is a muscular tube that connects the mouth to the stomach. Food is pushed along the oesophagus by rhythmic muscular contractions called peristalsis.

Stomach: The stomach is a J-shaped organ where food is mixed with gastric juice. Gastric juice contains hydrochloric acid and the enzyme pepsin which begins the digestion of proteins. The stomach also performs mechanical digestion by churning food into a semi-liquid form called chyme.

Digestive Glands

Salivary glands: These glands secrete saliva into the mouth. Saliva contains enzymes that start the digestion of carbohydrates.

Gastric glands: Present in the stomach wall, they secrete gastric juice that helps digest proteins.

Small Intestine and Accessory Organs

Small Intestine: The small intestine is the major site of digestion and absorption. It receives secretions from the liver and pancreas. The inner lining has many folds and villi which increase the surface area for absorption.

Pancreas: The pancreas secretes pancreatic juice containing enzymes (like trypsin, amylase and lipase) that digest proteins, carbohydrates and fats.

Liver and Bile: The liver produces bile which is stored in the gall bladder and released into the small intestine. Bile helps in emulsification of fats, making them easier for lipase to act on.

Absorption: The digested food (simple sugars, amino acids, fatty acids and glycerol) is absorbed by the villi of the small intestine and is transported to different parts of the body.

<!-- PAGE 4 -->
Large Intestine and Egestion

Large Intestine: The large intestine absorbs water and salts from the remaining undigested food. The remaining material is formed into faeces.

Egestion: The removal of undigested and unabsorbed food from the body in the form of faeces is called egestion.

Summary

Digestion involves both mechanical and chemical processes. Mechanical digestion includes chewing and churning. Chemical digestion involves enzymes that break down complex food molecules into simpler forms which can be absorbed and used by the body.
